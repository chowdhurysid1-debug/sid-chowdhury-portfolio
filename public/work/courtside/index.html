The study covers VR in general, not our product. We have no clinical data of our own yet. Full points: Hospitalized patients report more loneliness, which is linked to higher anxiety, depression and worse recovery. A 2025 meta-analysis found VR is safe and can reduce in-hospital anxiety and pain. Hospital TV, one-off sports-cart cameras and relaxation VR don't let patients feel present or be heard.

Unique value proposition

“For hospitals serving long-stay patients who can't get to live events, Courtside Connect is a patient engagement platform that streams live sports from in-stadium 360 degree cameras.”

Not only watching but feeling through connection.

Our positioning in the market is this: “READ SCREEN”

Vision and strategy

“No fan misses the game because of a hospital bed.”

2027: One launch venue and 3 free hospital pilots from May. 3 paid hospitals by December. 2028 to 2029: Sell to patient experience teams, starting with long-stay units. 22 hospitals and 5 venues. 2030 and beyond: 40 hospitals and 8 venues, then military morale programs and corrections.
Customer profile

Who buys and who uses

Quotes are from our mock expert interviews, not yet real customers.

Buyer wants to engage long-stay units within an existing entertainment budget. User is a fan who can't get to live events and wants to belong, not only to watch. Nurses hand out and clean the headsets, so it must be simple and infection safe.
Solution

A seat in the stadium, from bed

Business model

Hospitals pay $40 per bed per month. Teams and venues reach new fans.

Stadium: end-of-row 360 degree camera poles in designated seats, with a speaker, mic and fan signage. App: the live feed with AR chat, a scoreboard and switchable camera angles. Bedside: a shared pool of wipeable Quest 3S headsets checked out by nurses, plus TV mode for everyone else. Hospitals subscribe per bed, at $40 per bed per month. Teams and venues gain a new way to reach fans.
